for the Rip Off Game

1. Introduction

The following document is the 08968 AI ACW report for the AI and Flocking Controller for the Rip Off Game. There are four sections included in this report. The second section, entitled Analysis of AI Requirements for the Game, will describe the types of AI required by the game to create a complete game play. The next section, Design of AI Control Strategy for the Game, will discuss the works of designing the AI and the techniques applied, and how the goal is achieved. The Implementation and Testing section then explains how the testing progressed, including problems faced and how they were resolved. The Analysis of Results section will record the various results tested with the various AI controllers implemented, and contains ideas about how each controller can be improved. A Conclusion reflects on what has been achieved.

2. Analysis of AI Requirements for the Game

Rip-Off is an arcade game where the goal of the players is to protect the fuel canisters (cargo) seated in the centre of the screen from groups of invading Alien Craft that appear from the edges of the battlefield. The AI controller for the alien craft is aimed to rip off all the cargo protected by the player, as well as eliminate the player's craft which is on their way.

Basically, the behaviours for the alien crafts flow as follows: first navigating around the battlefield, then eventually finding the cargo or the player's craft. If they find the cargo, they navigate towards it and try to collect it — the alien craft needs to take a 180° turn to hook the cargo with the hooking device at the back of the craft. After successfully hooking the cargo, the alien craft tries to run away. If they meet the player's craft, they enter a battle state to shoot it. The result is either the alien craft shoots down the player and continues the cargo search, or the alien craft is eliminated by the player.

In order to determine what makes a successful AI controller, time was spent observing how the single-player mode was played. From that, ideas were built around different strategies players employ against the alien crafts. Most players prefer to guard the centre; some enjoy hit-and-run tactics. Introducing different alien craft AI controller spawning algorithms based on selected difficulty addresses the challenge of new players not being familiar with the craft's control scheme.

2.1 Requirements for AI Controller

For the single NPC AI controller, the aim is to create a controller that will be able to play a complete game with the player and produce a sensible result. Each controller will have its own goal and make its own decisions.

  • A Thief type focuses only on retrieving cargo and will not attack the player. This type acts kamikaze-style — straight-diving into the centre, hooking cargo, and retreating. It will not consider attacking the player's craft even when nearby. Suitable for early waves to give the player time to get familiar with the environment.
  • A Fighter type first focuses on attacking the player. After shooting down the player, it heads for the cargo. In difficult mode it will turn back to attack the player again if the re-spawned craft is close.
  • Both types used together within a single wave create a more challenging gameplay — some crafts attack the player while others aim for the cargo.
  • A simple separation algorithm should be added to avoid craft collisions. Crafts should maintain a certain distance from neighbours, avoiding two crafts being shot down by a single bullet and improving realism.

2.2 Requirements for Flocking Controller

For the flocking NPC AI controller, the aim is to create a group of AI controllers acting together. The navigation behaviours of the alien crafts now take into consideration all the alien crafts appearing in a wave. The flocking AI controller will have three new behaviours added to the architecture: cohesion, separation, and alignment.

  • Separation — each alien craft considers every other craft during the wave instead of only colliding pairs.
  • Alignment — ensures that all alien crafts keep the same rotation angle for navigation.
  • Cohesion — produces the steering force to move alien crafts toward the centre of mass of the group.

3. Design of AI Control Strategy for the Game

The aim of the AI control strategy is to create the AI controller based on the analysis of the requirements. The finite state machine (FSM) approach is applied as the fundamental design. The state machine is designed as a set of functions; the AI Controller class holds a pointer to a member function, m_stateFunction, that points to the current state. During the update process it directs the controller to the corresponding state function. 

// State function pointer in EweGameAiController
typedef void (EweGameAiController::*StateFunc)();
StateFunc m_stateFunction;

// Setting initial state on spawn
m_stateFunction = &EweGameAiController::stateNavigate;

Heuristics functions apply weighting to an alien craft's behaviours. Retrieving cargo earns more points than shooting down a player's craft. The distance between the craft and its target also affects motivation. For example, if the alien craft is much nearer to the player's craft, it will choose to attack even though cargo is worth more points.

3.1 Design for AI Controller

Two types of alien craft behaviours were created: the Thief, which only focuses on stealing cargo, and the Fighter, which first targets the player's craft. After defeating the player, the Fighter then seeks cargo.

When alien crafts are spawned, the game engine generates the controller type based on a craft generator factor influenced by the game difficulty: 

// Type generation on spawn
int factor = (rand() % (NUM_ALIENCRAFT + 1)) + m_difficulty;
m_AIType = factor % NUM_ALIENCRAFT;

After a Thief spawns, it looks for the nearest available cargo. A targeted cargo is marked so that no two crafts fight for the same one: 

Cargo* EweGameAiController::findNearestCargo() {
    if (m_cargoList.size() <= 0) return nullptr;

    // Initialise with the first cargo
    Cargo* nearest = m_cargoList.front();
    float minLen = (m_position - nearest->getPosition()).Length();

    for (Cargo* cargo : m_cargoList) {
        float tmp = (m_position - cargo->getPosition()).Length();
        if (tmp < minLen) {
            minLen = tmp;
            nearest = cargo;
        }
    }
    return nearest;
}

When a Thief arrives at the cargo position it aligns its back to the cargo to allow hooking. It then determines a retreat point: if it is near the top of the screen, it retreats upward; similarly for the bottom. The Fighter uses the same nearest-entity search for the player's craft and chases the player until it either shoots the player down or is itself eliminated.

When both Thief and Fighter are on the battlefield simultaneously, the Fighter covers the Thief by engaging the player, giving the Thief time to retrieve cargo. The player must choose between attacking the Fighter first (risking cargo loss) or the Thief first (risking being shot by the Fighter).

3.2 Design for Flocking Controller

One alien craft is selected as the leader; the rest become followers. The leader carries out the task of finding cargo or the player's craft. The followers follow the leader's movement using the three boid rules. The leader does not hook cargo directly — it issues a command to all followers to hook the cargo, then leads the group to the retreat point.

During the spawn phase, after all alien crafts have spawned and controllers have been set up, selectLeader() is called. The leader is assigned a distinct graphical layout (red colour) and its state is set to stateLeader.

The flocking controller relies on blending the three behaviours:

  • Cohesion — guides followers toward the leader's position. Implementation is simple: return the leader's world position as the target.
  • Separation — helps craft navigate slightly apart from other craft that are too close. The force applied is calculated as follows:
Vector2 EweFlockingAiController::separation() {
    Vector2 steeringForce(0.0f, 0.0f);

    for (AlienCraft* craft : m_group) {
        if (craft == m_ship) continue;

        Vector2 distance = m_position - craft->getPosition();
        float len = distance.Length();

        if (len > 0.0f && len <= m_separationRadius) {
            distance.Normalize();
            steeringForce += distance * ((m_separationRadius - len) / m_separationRadius);
        }
    }
    return steeringForce;
}
  • Alignment — the rotation angle of the followers is aligned to the leader. Returns the leader's current rotation angle.

If the leader is shot down by the player's craft, selectLeader() is called again to pick a new leader from the remaining followers. The remaining followers then cohese toward the new leader.

4. Implementation and Testing of AI Controllers

The individual AI controller is the fundamental part of the game AI. Most of the design and implementation code is reused for the flocking AI controller.

Three test cases were used throughout testing:

  • Player's craft stationary — observe how alien crafts play the game unimpeded.
  • Player's craft navigating — observe how alien crafts respond and how the game engine reacts to craft being shot down.
  • Full game — observe AI performance in an actual game.

4.1 Implementation and Testing for AI Controller

For the single NPC AI controller, implementation is straightforward. Every alien craft that spawns has its own independent controller. Updates to one controller do not affect others; each maintains its own state, navigation path, and target entity.

4.2 Implementation and Testing for Flocking Controller

Implementation of the flocking controller is significantly more complex than the individual controller. The seekPos function combines all three boid rules for followers, while the leader uses a direct pilot call: 

void EweFlockingAiController::seekPos(float tx, float ty) {
    if (m_isLeader) {
        m_ship->Pilot(1.0f, GetVectorAngle(m_ship->x, m_ship->y, tx, ty));
    } else {
        Vector2 sep = separation();

        // Cohesion offset + separation spread
        float cx = tx + sep.x * 55.0f;
        float cy = ty + sep.y * 55.0f;

        float angle = GetVectorAngle(m_ship->x, m_ship->y, cx, cy);

        // Alignment: nudge heading toward leader's rotation
        float delta = alignment() - angle;
        while (delta >  PI) delta -= TWO_PI;
        while (delta < -PI) delta += TWO_PI;
        angle += delta * 0.18f;

        m_ship->Pilot(1.0f, angle);
    }
}

The flocking behaviour was tested with increasingly large groups to identify separation force issues. With a large group, an incorrectly calculated separation force caused crafts to spin in place, unable to move out of the force field:

Craft Count 23456789

To resolve this, separation was excluded from the leader (the leader always leads; followers avoid the leader). A non-linear calculation method for separation among followers was then tested to produce better behaviour in large groups. The leader election and re-election on leader death was implemented as follows: 

void EweFlockingAiController::promoteToLeader() {
    m_isLeader    = true;
    m_stateFunction = &EweFlockingAiController::stateNavigate;
    g_world->m_flockLeader = m_ship;
}

// Called from World::UpdateEntities when the current leader dies
void World::electNewLeader() {
    for (EweFlockingAiController* ctrl : m_flockControllers) {
        if (ctrl->m_ship->isAlive() && !ctrl->isLeader()) {
            ctrl->promoteToLeader();
            return;
        }
    }
}

5. Analysis of Results

With the chosen test cases, Rip-Off was run several times to observe AI behaviour and identify improvements. Based on the results, most incorrect behaviours were fixed and enhancements were made to improve the gaming experience.

5.1 Analysis for AI Controller

The individual AI controller produces realistic behaviours and is able to play a reasonable game with the player. As the game advances and more alien crafts are spawned, the challenge increases. The uncertainty of which type spawns adds interesting variation, and the combination of Thief and Fighter makes the player think before taking the next action.

One weakness is the lack of variety among alien craft types — players may grow bored with only three possible combinations. To enhance the experience, a third type was added:

  • Aggressive — focuses exclusively on eliminating the player's craft. It never steals cargo, acting as a support role that provides time for Thieves to retrieve cargo by keeping the player occupied.

However, the Aggressive type introduces a problem: if an entire wave spawns as Aggressive, no craft retrieves cargo. Because the game engine requires all crafts in a wave to either be shot down or retreat with cargo before the next wave spawns, an all-Aggressive wave could cause the game to stall in an endless cycle.

Future improvements could include: backstab behaviours, lure-and-disengage tactics, or multi-stage escort formations.

5.2 Analysis for Flocking Controller

The flocking AI controller takes longer to complete a game than the individual controller with the same number of crafts, because only the followers retrieve cargo (not the leader). Completion times remain stable on average:

Longer times are also caused by the separation force pushing crafts apart, causing some to take wider arcs to reach the cargo. The main known weaknesses are:

  • Crafts can become stuck in an endless separation loop, preventing forward movement in large groups.
  • The leader does not wait for all followers to collect cargo before retreating.
  • The flock is predictable — all followers perform the same leader-issued command simultaneously.

Potential improvements include: a surround command that instructs followers to form a circle around the player and fire; issuing different commands to different followers (one retrieves cargo, another attacks the player); more varied formation patterns.

In general, the individual AI controller is more suitable for new players as each craft acts independently. The flocking controller is more suitable for experienced players who can handle coordinated group tactics. Among all controllers implemented, the individual AI controller is considered the best for the game overall, as it is more complete and brings more uncertainty to the game.

6. Conclusion

The ACW provided the opportunity to design and implement game AI for a game developed in another module. With the code base set up for the Rip-Off game, it was possible to fully concentrate on the AI code. Lots of knowledge and concepts of designing and implementing game AI were learned throughout the module.

The flocking algorithm was implemented to demonstrate the three basic boid behaviours. However, minor flaws remain — particularly in large groups — that were not resolved within the available time.

Overall the ACW produced satisfying results. The individual AI controller is stable and complete; the flocking controller works well with small groups and has clear paths for future enhancement.
